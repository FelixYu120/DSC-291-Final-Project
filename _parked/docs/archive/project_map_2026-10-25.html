Part 1

The one idea

A field record gives us two things: where and when the maize grew, and how much grain came out of it. From the where-and-when we fetch a season of free Sentinel-2 images (that is X). From the harvest we get one number in tonnes per hectare (that is Y). A model learns X→Y on some zones and is scored on zones it never saw.

0.15–0.30realistic field-level R² at 10 m (Burke & Lobell's resolution test)

Part 3

How one field becomes X and Y

This is the matching step, drawn on a real Malawi 2023 field from the decision report. The same seven steps run for every record in every cohort; only the pixel rule changes between polygons and points.

The seven steps, per record

  1. Fix the window by agro-zone: Kenya Feb–Sep, Malawi and Zimbabwe Oct–Jun. Never anchor on the crop-cut date.
  2. Search the catalogue (Earth Search or Planetary Computer) for the Sentinel-2 tile and window; keep one scene per date.
  3. Read the pixels: polygon interior for Malawi, 3×3 around the point elsewhere. Record how many.
  4. Fix the radiometry: reflectance = (DN + offset) / 10 000, offset −1000 where the scene is baseline 04.00 or later and still raw.
  5. Mask clouds with SCL classes 0, 1, 3, 8, 9, 10 and dilate one pixel. Count clear pixels per month.
  6. Composite per month: median of clear observations. An empty month gets mask = 1 for every band and for NDVI.
  7. Write three features: the Presto tensor, monthly GCVI/NDVI for the baselines, a peak-season chip for MOSAIKS. One row in the manifest.
The −1000 offsetProviders disagree on whether it is applied. Verify on one 2019 Kenya scene and one 2023 Malawi scene before bulk extraction.
Tile cloud cover liesIt describes a 110 km granule, not a 0.5 ha field. Use per-pixel SCL, never the tile percentage alone.
Point ≠ fieldA GPS point may be the homestead. Treat point cohorts as "location context" until providers answer; Malawi measures what 30 m of error costs.
Mid-season gapsJan–Feb in Malawi and Jan–Mar in Zimbabwe may have no clear scene. Presto masks them; baselines need an explicit rule.
Part 4

The experiment ladder: X, Y and the gate at every step

Steps run in this order. Every step uses the same split files and the same radiometry, so results are comparable. The gate column says what we decide and what happens on fail.

0week 0

Pilot: prove the pairing on 48 records

25 Oct – 1 Nov

X in

  • Chips and monthly composites for 12 Malawi, 12 Kenya, 16 Zimbabwe 2024, 8 Lacuna records
  • One Presto tensor per record
  • An inspection card each: label, scene ids, true-colour overlay, clear counts per month, NDVI curve

Y and what we measure

  • Y: the label with its unit and provenance stated
  • Pairing: label, unit, season and location all unambiguous
  • Coverage: clear pixels per field and month counted
  • Tensor: band order, offset, masks match known inputs
  • Trainability: Presto runs, loss finite, encoder gets gradients, 48 records can be overfit

Gate

PASScohort goes to full extraction; window and coverage rule frozen
FAILrepair within the week or drop that cohort; never slip the schedule
1week 1

Freeze the splits, run the baselines

2 – 8 Nov

X in

  • Monthly band medians and clear-count flags
  • Lat/lon, zone or district, planting month (for the location-only baseline)

Y and what we measure

  • Y: yield in t/ha, within cohort
  • RMSE (primary), MAE, R² against the training mean
  • Spatial group k-fold: clusters ≤ 1 km, at least 30 test groups, hyperparameters tuned inside training folds only
  • Models: mean, location-only, index ridge, XGBoost (later MOSAIKS, GRU)

Zfallback

If H1 fails: zone-mean yields with prediction-powered inference

X in

  • The same cross-fitted satellite predictor, never trained on the calibration zone
  • Per zone: n labelled fields resampled, N = 4n treated as unlabelled

Y and what we measure

  • Y: the zone mean yield
  • 95 % interval coverage of the full-sample zone mean; interval width; effective-sample-size gain
  • Expected: +9 / +19 / +32 % at within-zone R² 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3

Why it is still a paper

It answers how many crop cuts an insurer or ministry can save per zone. A null here still tells them something.

Part 5

What we measure, exactly

QuantityFormula or ruleWhy it is here
RMSE (t/ha)sqrt(mean((pred − y)²))Primary. Targets the mean, so it aggregates to zone means.
MAE, biasmean|pred − y| ; mean(pred − y)Bias overall and per zone shows systematic over- or under-prediction.
Out-of-sample R²1 − MSE / MSE(training mean)Against the training mean, never the test mean. Within-zone R² reported next to pooled.
Noise floorMSE_floor = mean((cut1 − cut2)²) / 4The best any model can do against a two-cut label. Zimbabwe 2024 and Malawi 2022 have two cuts.
Two-cut reliabilityρ₂ = 2r / (1 + r)Highest squared correlation any predictor can reach; r is the correlation between the two cuts.
Corrected r²r(f,c1) · r(f,c2) / r(c1,c2)Twice-Is-Nice correction, with out-of-fold predictions f.
Zero detectionAUROC, Brier score42 % of Zimbabwe 2024 is zero; RMSE on positive-yield fields reported separately.
Paired differencemean over groups of mean(L_A − L_B), t(G−1)Per-field loss differences averaged within test groups; cluster bootstrap for RMSE and R² differences.
TargetingSpearman ρ ; AUC(bottom 20 %) ; exclusion errorAid uses only the order. Judged against admin-mean, peak-NDVI and random ranking.
PPI gainESS × 1 / (1 − R² · N/(n+N))Effective-sample-size multiplier; comparable with Li et al.'s +23 %.
